Root canal treatment (endodontics)

Endodontics treats the inside of the tooth: the pulp, often called the nerve, and the root canals. The aim is to save your tooth, even when the pulp is affected.

When is it needed?

When the pulp is irreversibly inflamed (spontaneous pain, pain that wakes you at night, lasting sensitivity to heat), when it is necrotic, in case of an abscess, or after a blow to the tooth.

Root canal treatment

Under local anaesthetic, the pulp is removed completely. The canals are cleaned, disinfected and shaped, then sealed. An X-ray checks the result. Treatment takes one or two appointments depending on the case.

After treatment

Some discomfort when chewing for a few days is normal. A root-treated tooth is more fragile: it must then be restored with a filling, an onlay or a crown, depending on how much is left.

Retreatment

If a root-treated tooth becomes infected again, the treatment can often be redone: this is called endodontic retreatment.

A living tooth is always better

Whenever possible, root canal treatment is avoided: with deep decay and a pulp that is still healthy, Biodentine can keep the tooth alive (see the guide on tooth decay).

Further information

Endodontics, the branch of dentistry dealing with the inside of the tooth, aims to treat and prevent infections around the tips of the roots (periapical infections). The dentist uses it when the tooth can no longer stay alive, whether it is already necrotic or at risk of becoming so: this is known as root canal treatment.

The treatment consists of

  • To remove from the dental pulp, at the heart of the tooth, the remaining living tissue that is infected or likely to become so.
  • Cleaning the inside of the tooth: scraping the root canals with endodontic files, combined with sodium hypochlorite irrigation.
  • Shaping the canals down to the apical foramen, so that the irrigating solution reaches everywhere.
  • Drying the root canal system.
  • Filling the root canals.

In most cases, a local anaesthetic makes the procedure painless. The dentist then opens the tooth through its biting surface to reach the pulp.

Restoring the tooth

The tooth must then be rebuilt: with a filling if it is still strong enough, or with a crown if it is badly damaged. The aim is to keep the treatment sealed and prevent bacteria from getting back into the root.
