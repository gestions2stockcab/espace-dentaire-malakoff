Orthodontics for children

Children are generally referred for orthodontics from about the age of 6. This is when the first permanent incisor and first permanent molar come through.

In diagrams: the effect of thumb-sucking

Orthodontics, part of dentofacial orthopaedics, deals specifically with misalignment of the dental arches and teeth. By preventing or correcting malformations, dentofacial orthopaedics not only improves patients’ social well-being but, above all, protects oral health and therefore general health. Balanced, harmonious function of the chewing system keeps it healthy and delays the effects of natural ageing.

At this age, the orthodontist can already more or less predict how the jaws will grow (a mismatch between the upper and lower jaw, for example), which will shape the future profile. So-called interceptive treatments can act on growth to guide it.

Some recommend a first visit from the age of 4, especially in cases of mouth breathing or habits that cause deformities (thumb sucking, for example). Your orthodontist has various kinds of appliances to treat these functional problems. They may also work with a speech therapist when functional re-education is recommended.

The aim is to encourage harmonious growth, stop an anomaly from developing and make later treatment simpler, or in some cases unnecessary.

That is why, if you notice misaligned teeth or any other problem in your child, or if your dentist advises it, it is a good idea to make an appointment for an assessment and suitable treatment.
