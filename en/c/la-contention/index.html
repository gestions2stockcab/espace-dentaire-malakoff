Retention

An orthodontic retainer is a device that maintains, over time, the results achieved during the active phase of orthodontic treatment. Without a retainer, the teeth move on their own once the appliance has been removed for good. Teeth generally tend to return to their original position, but sometimes a new anomaly appears. A retainer is therefore essential to prevent relapse and ensure the treatment succeeds.

In diagrams: why a retainer is needed

What types of orthodontic retainer are there?

A retainer is either fixed or removable, and how long it is worn depends on the case. Here are the main types of orthodontic retainer.

The retainer wire

This is a rigid metal wire bonded to the inner surface of the front teeth, from canine to canine. Very discreet and easy to tolerate, it is often worn permanently by adults after treatment.

Orthodontic aligners

A clear aligner makes an excellent retainer. Removable, it does not move the teeth but maintains the alignment achieved by treatment.

The palatal plate

This is a removable retainer made of a resin plate that rests against the palate and a metal wire across the visible surface of the upper incisors and canines. It is reserved for young children and worn for a limited time.

If your retainer breaks or is lost, it is essential to contact the practice quickly so that it can be replaced or repaired as soon as possible.
