Gum care, periodontics

Healthy gums are pink and firm and do not bleed when you brush.

Warning signs

Bleeding, swollen or red gums, persistent bad breath, loose teeth or receding gums: see us without delay.

The cause

Plaque and tartar build up under the gum and inflame it: this is gingivitis. Without treatment, the inflammation can reach the bone holding the teeth: this is periodontitis. Smoking and diabetes increase the risk.

Treatment

Deep cleaning (scaling and root planing), learning the right brushing technique with interdental brushes, then regular follow-up. In some cases, gum surgery is needed.

Further information

Periodontics, as we practise it at Espace dentaire Malakoff, is used both to prevent and to treat gum disease, with suitable procedures such as root planing or gum grafts. Your periodontist helps you protect the tissues that support your teeth, slow down gum recession and loosening, and regain a healthy smile that lasts.

At the centre, periodontal care is provided by Dr Bénédicte Rioual Vergnol.

Healthy gums

When it is healthy, the gum never bleeds: not when eating, not when brushing, and not for no reason. It stays pink, without swelling or pain. Its immune defences work fully and protect it against bacteria and viruses alike.

The first signs

Bleeding, painful or swollen gums: you should see a dentist promptly. These signs show that bacteria have reached the space under the gum and are starting to damage the deeper tissues. Most often, the cause is dental plaque, also called biofilm. Left untreated, it can lead to gingivitis, then periodontitis.

Treating gum disease

First step: adopt strict oral hygiene, removing all plaque every day and using interdental brushes in narrow spaces. Initial treatment then tackles the direct causes (plaque, tartar) and indirect causes (occlusal forces, retention areas…): polishing, ultrasonic or hand scaling, root planing, treatment of decay, rebalancing of occlusal forces and splinting of mobile teeth that can still be kept.

Periodontal surgery depends on how much tissue has been destroyed. It may involve the gum alone, or also the alveolar bone: the aim is then to eliminate bony pockets, remove bacteria present deep down and give the gum tissue a functional shape again.

Regular check-ups

Without rigorous daily cleaning, periodontal disease may progress again. Hence the importance of regular follow-up.
